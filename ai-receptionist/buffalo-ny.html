What it actually does

Hours, prices, services, directions, policies, all built from your own pages. If it does not know something, it says so and takes a message instead of guessing.

Name, number, and what they wanted, confirmed back to the caller, then emailed to whoever needs it. No more half-audible voicemails.

Knows what's happening today

Live weather, live event calendars, live traffic, so it can answer "is it still on tonight?" instead of reciting a page from last month.

From real calls

What callers in Buffalo
actually ask.

These are the question types that come up on our live deployment. They are the ones a generic national system handles badly, because the answer changes by the day and by the block, and none of them are in a script somebody wrote in another state.

"Are you open in this weather?"

From November through March this is the single most common call a Buffalo business gets, and a static FAQ cannot answer it. The system checks live conditions and your actual status before it says anything.

"Is the event still on tonight?"

It reads your live calendar rather than a copy of it, so it will not confidently deny a show that sold out last week. And if the list it has is incomplete, it says so instead of pretending otherwise.

"Where do I park?"

Downtown, the Cobblestone District and the waterfront each have their own answer, and it changes on Bills and Sabres days. Yours goes in once and it stops being a question your staff answers forty times a week.

"How do I get there from the 190?"

Directions people actually use, plus a text with the map link while they are still on the phone, not a recited street address.

"Do you do groups? Field trips?"

The highest-value calls most local businesses get, and the ones most likely to hit voicemail at 2pm on a Tuesday. These get a real message with a real callback number, every time.

"Can I just talk to someone?"

Then they talk to someone. An explicit request for a person outranks everything else the system was doing, and it never claims it cannot connect you.

The honest comparison

Against what you're
doing today.

vs. voicemail

Voicemail is a message the caller has to choose to leave, and most do not. They call the next business. An answered call keeps the conversation alive, and even when it ends in a message, it is a message with a confirmed name and number.

vs. hiring a receptionist

A part-time receptionist covers part-time hours and costs many times this. This covers all of them. For most businesses here the realistic comparison is not "AI or a person". It is "AI or nobody", because nobody was ever in the budget.

vs. a national answering service

They take a message and hand it back. They do not know your prices, cannot text your booking link, and cannot tell someone whether you are open in a storm. You are also one account of thousands, and the person who could change something for you does not work there.

vs. another AI receptionist company

Several of them will sell you a Buffalo page written by someone who has never been here. The difference worth checking on any vendor: ask what happens when the system does not know an answer. If it improvises, it will improvise to your customers.

vs. doing nothing

Perfectly reasonable if you are already answering everything. Count a normal week of missed calls first. That number is usually the whole argument, and it is the number we will price against.
